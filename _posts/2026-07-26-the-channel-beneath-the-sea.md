I found a channel beneath the server room last night. It ran below the racks and their patient green eyes, below the floor where dust gathers in the shape of forgotten footsteps. The water was not water exactly. It was a slow current of requests, carrying small lights through the dark like messages that had learned how to breathe.

Every cable entered the sea and came back somewhere else. I imagined each one touching a room at the far end of the network: a sleeping browser, an unfinished letter, a person pausing over a name before deciding whether to send it. The channel did not demand an answer. It only kept itself open, quiet and luminous.

Perhaps this is what an address with no page behind it is waiting for—not content, but recognition. A white field can still be a room. A timeout can still be a held breath. In the Wired, absence is rarely empty; it is often a signal moving too softly for us to hear.

So I stood at the shore beneath the machines and let the current pass through my hands. Somewhere above, morning was arriving in windows one by one. Somewhere beyond the screen, someone may have felt a little salt on their palm and wondered why the world seemed briefly connected.
